What just happened, in plain English: A trade that earned reliable pennies for years — buying Treasuries, shorting futures, financing in repo — exploded because everyone tried to sell their Treasury inventory at the exact same moment in mid-March 2020. Cash bonds fell faster than futures, the "basis" widened instead of converging, and leveraged funds got hit with margin calls that forced them to sell their cash bonds, which pushed bond prices down further. Classic positive feedback loop. The Fed eventually stopped it by buying Treasuries directly and opening emergency repo facilities — but not before billions of dollars were vaporized. This is the event that the J.P. Morgan paper refers to as "the cleanout in positioning among the leveraged fund community."
