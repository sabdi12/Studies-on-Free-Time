Section 01

What is Carry?
The cost of holding something

Imagine you borrow $100 from your friend at 5% interest per year and you use that money to buy a magical money-printing machine that prints 4% per year. Every year, you're paying $5 in interest but only earning $4. You're losing $1 a year just to hold that machine. That loss is called negative carry.

In the Treasury market, the same thing happens. You buy a 5-year Treasury bond that yields 4.15%, but you had to borrow the money to buy it at the overnight repo rate of 5.31%.

THE CARRY FORMULA
Carry = Bond Yield − Financing Rate
// With Feb 2024 numbers:
Carry = 4.15% − 5.31% = −1.16%
// You're LOSING 1.16% per year just to hold the bond
😊

Positive Carry

Bond yield > Financing rate. You're getting paid to hold. Like renting a house for more than your mortgage payment.

😰

Negative Carry

Bond yield < Financing rate. You're bleeding money every day. Current situation in Feb 2024.

🔒

Term Financing

Lock your financing rate for 1 year at 4.91% instead of rolling overnight. Reduces uncertainty but costs more than expected overnights.

Total Return = Carry + Price Change

Your profit or loss has exactly two parts. The carry (drip of money in or out each day) and the price change (did the bond go up or down in value?). You can write it like this:

THE COMPLETE RETURN EQUATION
Total Return = Carry + Price Return
// Carry: known if you lock up term financing
// Price Return: UNKNOWN until the future arrives
// This is the great uncertainty every investor faces
Einstein Moment: In physics, we split energy into kinetic and potential. In bonds, we split return into carry and price change. Both are conserved — no magic, just accounting.
Section 02

The Forward Price:
The breakeven that unlocks everything

You're making a bet. You'll pay $1 now. If you win, great. If you lose, you're out $1. But what if I told you: "if you don't lose more than 22 cents, you still break even"? That 22 cents is your breakeven — and that's exactly what the forward price tells you in the bond market.

Here's the beautiful logic. If you lock up your financing for 1 year, you know exactly what carry you'll earn (or lose). The forward price is just the price the bond would need to be at in 1 year for your total return to be exactly zero.

SOLVING FOR ZERO TOTAL RETURN
0 = Carry + Price Return
// Rearranging:
Price Return needed = −Carry
// With negative carry (−1.16%/year), the bond must RALLY
// to offset the financing cost you're paying
Forward Yield = 3.93%  (vs Spot 4.15%)
// Bond must rally 22bp → that's your breakeven
Think of it like a road trip. You're driving against the wind (negative carry). The "forward destination" is where you need to reach just to break even on fuel. Go further than that? You're ahead. Stop short? You lost money.

The Relationship Between Carry and Forward Direction

📉

Negative Carry → Forward Yield Lower

Bond must rally (yield falls) to overcome carry loss. Forward yield < Spot yield.

📈

Positive Carry → Forward Yield Higher

You're getting paid to hold, so bond can sell off a bit and you still profit. Forward yield > Spot yield.

Swaps: The Same Logic

In interest rate swaps, you receive a fixed rate and pay floating (SOFR). This is identical to a financed Treasury position. The 1y4y swap rate is the swap equivalent of the 1-year forward on the 5-year Treasury — it's the 4-year rate, starting 1 year from now.

SWAP ANALOGY
Long 5y Treasury (financed) ≡ Receive 5y Swap Fixed
1y Forward on 5y Tsy ≡ 1y4y Swap Rate
// Same math, different wrapper

Section 04

Curve Trades:
When you bet on the shape, not just the level

Imagine two thermometers — one for New York (5y) and one for Miami (30y). Instead of betting on whether it gets hotter or colder, you're betting on the temperature difference between the two cities. That's a curve trade: you go long one bond and short another, and you only care about the spread between them.

The 5-30 Steepener Example

From the BofA data (Feb 9, 2024):

STEEPENER TRADE MATH
Buy 5y (yield 4.15%) + Sell Short 30y (yield 4.38%)
─────────────────────────────────────
5y breakeven: must rally > 22bp to profit
30y breakeven: must NOT rally > 3bp to profit
─────────────────────────────────────
Net: 5-30 slope must steepen ≥ 19bp
// Spot slope: 23bp → Forward slope: 42bp
// The forward is already pricing 19bp of steepening!
Key Insight: The 5-30 steepener is a negative carry trade. The market's forward price already assumes 19bp of steepening. You only profit if it steepens more than that.

Duration Weighting: Why it Matters

A 30y bond moves much more in price for every 1bp change in yield than a 5y bond. If you buy $1M of 5y and short $1M of 30y, you have way more risk from the 30y side. Duration weighting means adjusting the notionals so that a 1bp move affects both sides equally — then only the spread of yield changes matters, not each individually.

Non-Duration-Weighted

P&L depends on 5y yield AND 30y yield separately. Messy and hard to analyze.

Duration-Weighted

P&L depends ONLY on the yield difference (spread). Clean and focused.

The BofA Example

5-30 duration-weighted steepener needs 19bp of steepening to break even. Simple and elegant.

Section 05

Forwards as Building Blocks:
The 10y rate is 9 forwards stacked up

Think of a 10-year interest rate as a tower of LEGO bricks. Each brick is a 1-year forward rate. The tower's height is the 10y rate. If you want to change just one brick in the middle without touching the top, you use a forward instrument. With a spot trade, you're moving the whole tower.

No-arbitrage logic means the 10y rate must equal the compounded chain of shorter forwards:

NO-ARBITRAGE COMPOUNDING
(1 + r_10y)^10 = (1+r_1y) × (1+f_1y1y) × ... × (1+f_9y1y)
// Where f_Xy1y = 1-year rate, X years forward
// If this breaks → arbitrage → traders push it back

Why Trade the 5y5y Instead of the 10y?

🎯

10y Spot Rate

You're implicitly trading BOTH near-term Fed cut timing AND long-run terminal rate. Two views bundled into one.

🔬

5y5y Forward

You're expressing a pure view on the terminal Fed rate (where cuts stop). Near-term Fed timing has minimal effect.

⚗️

Einstein Principle

Forwards let you isolate variables, like a controlled experiment. The spot rate is a confounded experiment.

Deep Insight: Whether forward overnight rates represent true Fed expectations or include a "term premium" doesn't change the math. The compounding relationship holds regardless. Term premium is a separate layer of interpretation.
Section 06

Rolldown:
Time travel for your bond

Imagine you buy a bottle of wine that will be worth more as it gets older. Rolldown is when you buy a 3-year bond, and one year later it has "aged" into a 2-year bond — and if the yield curve hasn't moved, that 2-year bond yields less (prices more) than the 3-year did. You made money just by waiting. The curve did the work for you.

Rolldown assumes the yield curve stays exactly the same. If the 2y yields 4.6% and 3y yields 4.4% today, rolldown says: next year, the 2y will still yield 4.6%. Your 3y bond (now a 2y) will have appreciated in price because it moved from 4.4% to 4.6%... wait, that's a loss!

ROLLDOWN FORMULAS FOR A 5Y NOTE
1y Rolldown = 5y spot − 4y spot
1y Carry Breakeven = 5y spot − 1y4y forward
1y Carry + Rolldown = 4y spot − 1y4y forward
// Rolldown is best when the curve is steep (big 5y-4y spread)
// And when the Fed is firmly on hold (curve stable)

Section 07

Test Yourself:
Are you Einstein yet?

① The 5y Treasury yields 4.15% and the 1y financing rate is 4.91%. An investor goes long the 5y. What is the carry?
Carry = Bond Yield − Financing Rate = 4.15% − 4.91% = −0.76%. You're paying 4.91% to borrow money that earns only 4.15%. The −1.16% answer would apply if using the overnight SOFR rate of 5.31% instead of the 1y term rate.
② If carry is negative, the 1-year forward yield of the bond will be _____ than the spot yield.
With negative carry, you're losing money every day you hold. For the total return to be zero, the bond must appreciate in price — which means the yield must fall. So the forward yield is lower than spot. The bond "needs to rally" to overcome the carry loss.
③ An investor thinks the Fed will cut rates faster than the market expects (term financing rate is too high). They have a long 5y position. What should they prefer?
If you think the Fed cuts faster than priced, overnight rates will fall faster than the market implies. By rolling overnight financing, you'll pay a lower actual financing cost than the locked-in 1y term rate of 4.91%. The spot trade is better when you think term financing is overpriced.
④ The 5-30 spot slope is 23bp. The 1y forward 5-30 slope is 42bp. A duration-weighted 5-30 steepener needs to steepen how much to break even?
The forward already prices in 42bp of slope, vs the current 23bp. So the market is already expecting 19bp of steepening over 1 year. Your steepener trade only profits if steepening exceeds that 19bp threshold. The breakeven is the forward slope minus the spot slope: 42 − 23 = 19bp.
⑤ Why might a trader prefer to trade the 5y5y swap forward instead of the 10y spot rate?
The 10y spot rate is a blend of the 5y spot rate (sensitive to near-term Fed cut timing) and the 5y5y forward (sensitive to the terminal/long-run Fed rate). If you only have a view on the terminal rate, trading the 10y spot forces you to take on unwanted exposure to near-term Fed timing. The 5y5y isolates that specific view cleanly.
