📍 Sticky Delta (Stochastic Vol)

Key Principle: The smile is anchored to delta (moneyness), not absolute price levels.

When Spot Moves: The entire volatility curve shifts to remain centered on the new spot in delta space.

Quote Stability: The implied volatility for "25-delta call" remains roughly constant because delta automatically recalibrates to the new spot.

Model Logic: Volatility is pure randomness layered on top—independent of spot level.

🎯 Local Volatility: Price-Level Dependent

Key Principle: Volatility is tied to specific price levels, not to relative moneyness.

When Spot Moves: The smile stays anchored to fixed price levels. What was the ATM strikes changes.

Quote Behavior: The implied volatility for a fixed strike (say $105 call) remains similar, creating a "sticky strike" effect.

Model Logic: Volatility depends on the current state—σ(S, t)—creating state-dependent risk.

🧠 Understanding the Mechanics

Pure Stochastic Volatility:
  • The model specifies: dS = μS dt + σ_t S dW_S, where σ_t follows its own process
  • Volatility is orthogonal to spot level—it doesn't depend on where you are in price space
  • The smile shape is purely a result of volatility randomness, not spot dependency
  • As spot drifts, the smile "walks" with it in delta space—same relative positioning
  • Result: Sticky delta—moneyness-based quotes stay stable
Local Volatility:
  • The model specifies: dS = μS dt + σ(S,t) S dW, where vol is deterministic and state-dependent
  • Different price levels have different volatilities built into the model
  • The smile is locked to the price axis, not to relative moneyness
  • As spot moves, the ATM point moves, but strikes remain fixed—changing their moneyness
  • Result: Sticky strike—absolute price level quotes stay more stable
